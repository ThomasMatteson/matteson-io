    Giving software a job

    What a coworker
    needs to do real work.

    A digital coworker isn’t a chat window. It is given a job, the same way a new hire is: what it is responsible for, what it can use, and who to bring in.

    A defined responsibility

    A clear part of the work it owns, from start to handoff.

    Relevant knowledge

    The customers, procedures and history that bear on that work.

    Permitted tools

    The systems it may use, and what it may do in each.

    A record of unfinished work

    Everything still open, so nothing depends on memory.

    A way to involve people

    Who to ask, who decides, and how to reach them.

    For example, a service coworker’s responsibility could include understanding incoming requests, checking the site and equipment, identifying missing information, coordinating the next authorized step, and following the job through its part of the process.

    A vague email arrives with a photo attached. Follow the service coworker as it turns that into a job, notices what’s missing, asks for it, and picks up exactly where it left off when the answer comes back days later.

                Built to be trusted

                Coworkers you can
                actually hand things to.

                Four rules from the agentic architecture make a coworker you can trust with real work.

                Limits

                They know where to stop

                Authority is set by people. Above the line, a coworker stops and asks a named person.

                Approval bound to the action
                Authority

                They never hold the keys

                A coworker proposes. A separate control layer holds the accounts and acts only on what was approved.

                Permission lives in the control layer
                Handoffs

                They hand off cleanly

                Owner, approval, evidence and next step move with the work. Nothing falls between two coworkers.

                The protected transition
                Record

                They show their work

                Every action is recorded: who asked, who approved, what changed and what proves it.
