You’re at a facility you have never visited. You know the customer has a problem. You may not know where the equipment is, which version it is, what happened last visit, or what is unusual about this site. The experience begins before the repair.

What it sees, and what it can’t

Three kinds of evidence,
brought together.

The system combines what the person sees with information the person can’t directly see. Each source contributes something different, and the experience is honest about which is which.

The camera

What is visible

The equipment, its components and labels, the condition of parts, where the technician is and what they’re working on.

Connected sensors

What can’t be seen

Temperature, pressure, vibration, gas and other conditions, measured by instruments on the equipment or on site.

The job record

What should be happening

The work order, the approved scope, the procedure and the requirements this job has to meet.

A camera can’t detect gas.

Understanding the scene doesn’t reveal an invisible leak. A gas sensor supplies that measurement; the glasses help locate it and guide the person away from it.

Seeing a lock isn’t proof of isolation.

Recognizing a lockout device shows it is there. Confirming hazardous energy is isolated takes the right readings and the procedure’s own checks.

Vibration

Gets attention

Reaches someone who is looking somewhere else, or working somewhere too loud to hear.

Audio

Explains

Says what the issue is and what to do, without the person having to stop and read.

Visual overlay

Locates

On suitable hardware, points to the component or area that matters, right where it is.

A working companion

Ask it anything
about the job.

The system answers from the identified equipment, the approved procedures, the job’s requirements and the evidence so far. When information conflicts, or the situation is outside what it reliably knows, it says so and brings in a person who does.

Documentation, done on site

Get it right while
you’re still standing there.

The system knows what this job needs to be documented: which component to photograph, from which angle, and which readings to take. It checks each capture for clarity and content as it happens.

    Learning and doing

    From practice to the field,
    and back again.

    The same procedure, terminology and evidence requirements follow a person from rehearsal into real work. And what the field learns can make its way back to everyone.

    Rehearse

    Practice the procedure in a simulated environment, with the same guidance used on site.

    Perform

    Receive guidance while performing an authorized task on real equipment.

    Reflect

    Get feedback afterwards on what went well and what was missed.

    Propose

    A useful discovery on a real job is captured as a proposed improvement.

    Review and share

    The person who owns that knowledge reviews it. Approved improvements become available to everyone.

    Observed behavior doesn’t automatically become a company rule. Every lasting change to a procedure goes through a person who owns that knowledge.

    Keeping the work safe

    See. Judge.
    Then gate the work.

    Every step, every photo and every job advances on proof the system has checked.

    See

    The camera in the glasses, a phone or a tablet captures what is actually in front of the technician.

    Fuse

    Readings from gas, pressure, air-quality and equipment sensors are combined with what the camera sees.

    Judge

    AI checks the scene and the readings against the job’s requirements and the safety conditions.

    Gate

    If a check fails, the step stays locked. Work, photos and closure wait until the condition is met.

    What it does

    Three jobs,
    one pair of glasses.

    Switch modes to see what appears in the lens: guidance on the equipment, a safety gate before the work, and proof checked on the spot.

    Closing a job

    The system closes
    the job.

    Five checks run before a work order can close, each one done by the system while the crew is still on site.

    Work confirmed

    AI confirms from the photo that the right work was done.

    Lockout confirmed

    Sensors confirm the lockout is in place and valid.

    Photos checked

    Every photo is checked the moment it is taken.

    Proof and safety pass

    Closure opens once both conditions are met.

    Fixed on site

    Anything missing is caught and corrected before the crew leaves.

    How it’s built

    From the lens
    to the control room.

    Three layers. Decisions that can’t wait happen on site; the heavy lifting happens in the cloud. Sensors across the location feed one picture of what is happening.

    On the wearer

    Glasses, phone or tablet

    A display over the field of view, a camera and depth sensors, motion sensing, and optional biometrics for stress and fatigue.

    On site

    Edge processing

    Computer vision and the safety engine run close to the work, so a hazard warning never waits on a signal from far away.

    In the cloud

    Control and records

    Model updates, work orders, analytics, remote supervision and a complete record of what was verified.

    Where it reaches

    Anywhere work must
    be proven before it counts.

    Written for field operations, and broad enough for any work where safety and proof matter more than a signature.
